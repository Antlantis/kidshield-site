KidShield Privacy Policy

Effective date: September 23, 2026 Developer: Anthony Craig ("we", "us") — ant.sd.shield@gmail.com

KidShield is a parental-control app for iPhone and iPad. A parent installs it on their child's device to block apps and websites, set time limits and a bedtime, and — if the parent chooses — check on the device from the parent's own phone. This policy explains what information KidShield handles, where it goes, and the choices you have. We wrote it to be read, not skimmed: it is short because the app collects very little.

The short version

  • KidShield has no accounts, no advertising, no analytics, no tracking, and no third-party SDKs. We never sell or share data.
  • Most of what KidShield does happens entirely on the child's device, using Apple's Screen Time framework. We cannot see which apps or websites you chose to restrict.
  • If you pair a child device with a parent phone, a small amount of status information (and, only if you turn it on, the device's location) is stored in Apple's iCloud (CloudKit) so your phone can display it — encrypted end-to-end with a key derived from your pairing code, so it is unreadable to us and to Apple.
  • We do not collect message contents, browsing history, keystrokes, photos, contacts, passwords, or per-app usage history.

Who KidShield is for

KidShield is designed to be set up and controlled by a parent or legal guardian. It is installed on a child's device that belongs to the parent's Family Sharing group and must be authorized with the parent's Apple Account password. Any information about a child that KidShield handles is collected at the direction of, and is only visible to, that parent. Children cannot create accounts or provide information to us directly.

What stays on the device

The following is stored only on the device it was entered on, in KidShield's private app storage, and is never sent to us:

  • Your rules: the apps, app categories and websites you selected to block or limit (these are stored as opaque Apple tokens — KidShield itself cannot read which apps they are), daily time budget, bedtime hours, web-filter settings, device-hardening settings, alert preferences.
  • The child's name or nickname you enter.
  • Your parent PIN, stored only as a one-way hash (it cannot be recovered from the stored value).
  • A local activity log (e.g. "bedtime started", "parent paused apps") that you can clear at any time.
  • "Today's usage" totals shown in the parent area are rendered by Apple's Screen Time report system inside a sandbox; KidShield cannot read, store or transmit them.

What is shared with your parent phone (optional pairing)

If you pair the child's device with the parent version of KidShield, the following is stored in Apple's iCloud using CloudKit so the parent's phone can read it:

  • Device status: the child's name/nickname you entered, a random device identifier generated by KidShield (not your Apple ID or hardware serial), the app version, whether the daily limit has been reached, whether bedtime is active, whether the parent has paused the device, whether Screen Time authorization is in place, the current rule settings (numbers and switches — not the app/website selections), and any pending "ask for more time" request including the name of the app or website the child asked about.
  • Events: alerts such as "daily limit reached", "bedtime started", "blocked attempt" or "asked for more time", with a time stamp and a short message that may include the name of the app or website involved.
  • Commands and rules the parent sends to the child device (pause, resume, extra minutes, rule changes).
  • Location — see the next section. Location sharing is off by default; nothing location-related is stored unless a parent turns it on.

End-to-end encryption. Before any of this leaves a phone, KidShield encrypts it (AES-256-GCM) with a key derived from your family's secret pairing code, and files it under a one-way hash of that code rather than the code itself. The pairing code is generated on the parent's phone and never sent to us or to Apple. As a result, the CloudKit database holds only unreadable data and random-looking tags: we cannot read your family's status, rules, alerts or location, and neither can Apple. The only things stored unencrypted are the random device identifier, the type of a command (for example "pause") and time stamps, which reveal nothing about your family.

Anyone who has the pairing code can pair with your family's devices and read this information, so treat it like a password: share it only with the child's other parent or guardian and only by typing it into KidShield. You can unpair at any time from the child device, and the parent phone can generate a new code at any time (Privacy & your data → Delete all family data).

Data stored in CloudKit is additionally encrypted in transit and at rest by Apple and is held in Apple's data centers, in a CloudKit database operated under our developer account. We do not access, sell or share it, and we have no business use for it; the only reason it exists is to move status between your own devices. Apple's handling of iCloud data is described in Apple's privacy policy (https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/).

Location

KidShield can show a child device's approximate location on the parent's phone. This feature works only when all of the following are true: the device is paired, the Location switch is on in KidShield, and location permission has been granted to KidShield in iOS on the child's device. Until then, no location is collected.

When enabled, KidShield records the device's most recent latitude/longitude, accuracy and time stamp when iOS reports a significant change of location and when the parent taps "Locate now". Only the most recent location is kept; each update replaces the last. Location is never shared with anyone other than the paired parent phone(s) and is never used for advertising or profiling.

KidShield asks for "Always" location permission because the parent needs to be able to locate the device while KidShield is not open. You can revoke this at any time in Settings → Privacy & Security → Location Services on the child device, or turn the Location switch off in KidShield; the parent's phone then stops showing a location.

Optional alert services you configure

KidShield can also send alerts to a notification service you choose (an "ntfy" topic or a webhook URL such as a Discord or Slack webhook). This is entirely optional and off unless you enter a URL. If you enable it, the event text described above (for example "Liam hit the daily limit") is sent directly from the child device to the address you entered. That service's own privacy policy applies to what happens after delivery. We do not operate any such service and never receive these messages.

Notifications

If you use the parent version of KidShield, Apple Push Notification service delivers alerts to your phone. Apple receives only the device push token and the alert content necessary to display the notification.

What we don't do

  • No account creation, email address, or sign-in.
  • No advertising, ad identifiers, or ad networks.
  • No analytics, crash-reporting or usage-measurement SDKs.
  • No selling, renting or sharing of personal information, ever.
  • No collection of message contents, browsing history, search history, keystrokes, photos, contacts, call logs or per-app usage history.
  • No use of data for any purpose other than the parental-control features you set up.

Children's privacy (COPPA and similar laws)

KidShield is a tool operated by parents, not a service directed at children. The only information about a child that leaves the child's device is the status, event and (optional) location information described above, and it is collected solely at the parent's direction and delivered solely to the parent. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children directly, we do not contact children, and we do not use any child's information for marketing, profiling or advertising. A parent can review this information in the parent app, stop collection by unpairing or disabling location, and request deletion at any time (see below).

Your choices and data deletion

  • Change or remove restrictions: in the child device's parent area (PIN protected).
  • Stop sharing status: Remote Control → Unpair on the child device. The device stops sending status, events and location immediately.
  • Stop sharing location: turn off the Location switch in KidShield or revoke location permission in iOS.
  • Delete everything on the device: delete the app; all local data goes with it.
  • Delete data stored in iCloud: unpairing a child device (Remote Control → Unpair) deletes everything that device stored. On the parent phone, each device has "Remove this device & delete its data", and Privacy & your data → "Delete all family data" removes every record for the family and issues a new pairing code. These run immediately, from the app, with no request to us needed. Because the data is encrypted with your code, anything left behind by a failed deletion is unreadable in any case.

Data retention

Device status and location records are overwritten each time the device reports; only the latest values are kept. Alert (event) records are kept for 30 days so the parent can review recent activity, then deleted automatically; you can delete them sooner as described above. Nothing is retained for our own purposes.

Security

All network traffic uses HTTPS/TLS. Family data is encrypted end-to-end on the phone with AES-256-GCM (see above) and again by Apple in transit and at rest. The parent PIN is stored only as a one-way SHA-256 hash. Restrictions on the child device are protected by Apple's Screen Time framework so the child cannot remove KidShield or its rules without the parent's Apple Account password.

California and other privacy rights

We do not sell or share personal information as defined by the California Consumer Privacy Act, and we do not use it for targeted advertising. Residents of California, the EU/UK and other jurisdictions with privacy laws may exercise their rights to access, correct or delete information by contacting us at ant.sd.shield@gmail.com. Because KidShield holds so little, in most cases the fastest way to exercise these rights is the unpair and delete steps above.

Changes to this policy

If we change how KidShield handles information, we will update this page and the effective date above, and, for material changes, describe them in the App Store release notes.
